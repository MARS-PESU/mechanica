Red Planet Strikers (Robo Soccer)

I. Objective

To design and operate a remote-controlled or semi-autonomous robot capable of navigating a field, controlling a ball, and scoring goals against an opponent.

II. Robot Specifications

  • Dimensions: The robot must fit inside a box of 300mm x 300mm x 300mm at the start of the match. Expansion after the start is permitted, provided it does not exceed 350mm in any direction. +-5% of tolerance is given.
  • Weight: Maximum weight must not exceed 3.5 kg (including battery).
  • Power Source: On-board power only. Maximum voltage allowed is 13V DC.
  • Locomotion: Can be wheeled, tracked, or legged. Use of suction pads or adhesives to "hold" the ball is strictly prohibited.
  • Ball Handling: The robot cannot "wrap" around the ball or encompass more than 50% of the ball's surface area or even stick itself to the ball/court. The ball must be free to be "stolen" by the opponent at all times.

III. Arena Specifications

  • Field Size: Approximately 10ft x 6ft, surfaced with green carpet or painted plywood.
  • Goal Posts: 600mm wide.
  • Ball: A standard tennis ball or a high-density foam ball (approx. 65mm diameter).

IV. Gameplay & Scoring

  • Format: 1v1 matches with maximum of two robots per team.
  • Duration: Two halves of 4 minutes each. A 2-minute "Repair Window" is provided between halves.
  • Scoring: 1 point per goal. In the event of a tie, a 2-minute "Golden Goal" period occurs. If still tied, a "Penalty Shootout" (3 shots each) follows.
  • Kick-off: The ball is placed at the center. Robots must be behind their respective goal lines until the whistle blows.

V. Fouls & Penalties

  • Yellow Card: Awarded for "pinning" an opponent against the wall for more than 5 seconds or for minor human interference.
  • Red Card: Awarded for intentional damage to the arena or the opponent’s internal circuitry. Result: Immediate disqualification.
  • Out of Bounds: If the ball leaves the arena, it is placed at the center of the half from where the ball fell off.

Titan Tussle (Robo Sumo)

I. Objective

Two robots compete in a circular ring (Dohyo). The goal is to push the opponent out of the ring while remaining inside.

II. Robot Specifications

  • Weight Class: Exactly 3.5 kg maximum. This is the most strictly enforced rule.
  • Dimensions: 300mm x 300mm x 300mm at the start. Once the match begins, robots may deploy wedges, arms, or ramps.
  • Materials: No magnets to increase downforce on a metal floor. No sticky tires that leave residue. sharp edges that might harm our floor are prohibited.
  • Weaponry: Non-destructive. Wedges, lifters, and pushers are encouraged. No spinning blades, flames, or projectiles.

III. The Dohyo (Ring)

  • Dimensions: A circular platform, 150cm in diameter, elevated off the ground.
  • Surface: Black finish with a 5cm wide White Border (Tawara).

IV. Match Rules

  • The Bow: Robots start 40cm apart, facing away from each other sideways.
  • The 5-Second Rule: After the "Start" command, there is a 5-second delay where no robot can move (safety/prep).
  • Victory: A robot wins a "Yuhkoh" point when: (1) The opponent touches the area outside the ring. (2) The opponent is flipped and cannot move.
  • Format: Best of 3 rounds. Each round has a 3-minute limit.

V. Disqualifications

  • Passive Play: If a robot avoids contact for more than 30 seconds, it receives a warning.
  • Damage: If a part falls off a robot (larger than 20 grams), the round is stopped and the robot may be disqualified if it cannot continue safely.
  • limit: If a team recieves more than 2 warning is disqualified

Olympus Dash (Robo Race)

Theme: The Martian Time Trials – High-Speed Solo Pursuit

I. Event Overview

The Olympus Dash is a technical sprint designed to test the limits of a robot’s power-to-weight ratio, traction control, and the operator's handling skills. Unlike a traditional grid race, this is a Time Attack format. Each robot faces the "Olympus Circuit" alone, battling only the clock.

II. Robot Technical Requirements

  • Dimensions: The robot must not exceed 350mm x 350mm x 350mm.
  • Weight: No minimum weight; maximum weight is 6kg.
  • Power: Maximum battery voltage is 18V DC. Only dry cell or LiPo/Li-ion batteries are permitted.
  • Drivetrain: Any number of wheels or tracks are allowed.
  • Safety: The robot must have an easily accessible "Kill Switch" or the operator must be able to cut power immediately via the remote.

III. Arena & Track Layout

The track is a 25-meter circuit consisting of:

  1. The Launch Pad: A 3-meter straight for initial acceleration.
  2. The Martian Ridges: A series of 15mm speed bumps to test suspension.
  3. The Dead Turn: A 180-degree hairpin curve with a radius of 50cm.
  4. The Regolith Pit: A 2-meter section of low-friction surface (simulated dust).
  5. The Summit: A 15-degree incline ramp followed by a 10cm drop-off.

IV. Tournament Format & Scoring

  • The "Two-Flight" Rule: Every registered robot is granted exactly two (2) official timed runs.
  • Best of Rounds: The final ranking is determined solely by the single fastest lap recorded across either of the two runs.
  • Preparation: Teams are given 2 minutes in the "Staging Area" to check battery levels and tire grip before their heat begins.
  • Timing: Time is recorded using a digital stopwatch or IR gate. The clock starts when the front axle crosses the start line and stops when the same point crosses the finish line.

V. Penalties & Disqualifications

  • Off-Track (Minor): If a robot leaves the track with two wheels but recovers, the timer continues.
  • Off-Track (Major): If the robot completely leaves the track, the run is paused. The operator must return the bot to the point of exit. A +5 second penalty is added to the lap time.
  • Human Touch: Any intentional human touch to the robot during a run (unless authorized by a referee for safety) results in a DNF for that specific run.

Labyrinth of Curiosity (Line Follower)

Theme: Navigating the Trenches – Autonomous Precision Trials

I. Event Overview

This is a test of autonomous logic and sensor calibration. Robots must follow a 2-3cm black line on a white carpeted surface. The "Labyrinth" contains complex geometries designed to confuse standard PID loops, requiring robots to be both fast and "smart."

II. Robot Technical Requirements

  • Autonomy: 100% Autonomous. No external communication allowed after the start.
  • Dimensions: Max footprint of 200mm x 200mm.
  • Sensors: No limit on the number of IR, LiDAR, or Color sensors.
  • Line Compatibility: The robot must be capable of tracking a line that fluctuates in width between 2cm and 3cm.
  • Braking: The robot must have a programmed "End of Line" detection to stop within 20cm of the finish mark.

III. The Course Architecture

The Path: Black vinyl tape on a white matte carpet.

The Challenges:

  • Acute Junctions: Turns sharper than 90 degrees.
  • The Discontinuity: A 5cm gap in the line (The "Signal Loss" zone).
  • The Intersection: A cross-junction where the robot must move straight without turning.
  • The Negative Path: A 1-meter section where the colors invert (White line on a black background).

IV. Tournament Format & Scoring

  • The "Three-Flight" Rule: Each robot is allowed three (3) official runs.
  • Best of Rounds: Only the fastest successful completion out of the three runs will be counted for the final leaderboard.
  • Run Procedure: (1) The Explorer: test sensors. (2) The Optimized: high-speed run. (3) The Final Dash: high-risk attempt.
  • Calibration: Teams have a 5-minute window in a "Practice Zone" to tune their code before their first run.

V. Penalties & Logic Failures

  • Line Loss: If the robot loses the line for more than 3 seconds, the run is terminated and recorded as a DNF.
  • Shortcutting: If a robot leaves the line and accidentally joins a later section of the track, that run is disqualified.
  • Hand of God: Each "Reset" (picking up the robot and placing it back on the line) incurs a +10 second penalty. Maximum 2 resets allowed per run.
